Why interesting

This is an experience-signature search lane rather than a conventional job-title lane: unstable or marginal switch-mode power supplies, compensation-network design/tuning, loop-gain measurement, Bode analysis, pole/zero placement, scripted or tool-assisted control simulation, and correlation back to real hardware.

The search is deliberately wider than the literal phrase Type II compensation. Current contract adverts more often describe the need as SMPS control loops, stability and compensation, control-loop tuning, stability analysis, PFC/DC-DC control loops, LTspice/PLECS, bring-up, validation, transient response, EMC/debug or power supply troubleshooting. The exact Type II/III vocabulary appears more often in specialist power-electronics design organisations and technical tooling than in general recruitment copy.

This is a useful contract niche because the underlying work is bounded and problem-shaped: a converter exists, its control behaviour is uncertain or inadequate, and a senior engineer can model, instrument, tune, validate and hand back a demonstrably stable implementation. It can therefore surface opportunities that would be missed by generic electronics engineer or power electronics engineer searches.

Working-style fit

Prioritise work where theory is tied directly to bench evidence rather than clean-sheet power-topology ownership alone. Strong signals include:

  • Type II / Type III compensation networks;
  • loop-gain / phase-margin / gain-margin analysis;
  • plant and compensator poles/zeros;
  • Bode plots and frequency-response injection/measurement;
  • transient-response optimisation;
  • current-mode or voltage-mode control;
  • buck / boost / flyback / PFC / isolated converter control loops;
  • analogue compensation and digitally implemented compensators;
  • LTspice, PLECS, PSIM, MATLAB/Octave/Python or equivalent scripted modelling;
  • comparison of simulated and measured loop behaviour;
  • unstable/oscillating supplies, poor damping, ringing or load-step problems;
  • control-loop interaction with PCB parasitics, component tolerances, EMI filters or real loads;
  • prototype bring-up, fault isolation and validation rather than simulation in isolation.

The preferred work shape is observe instability -> model/derive loop -> place/tune poles and zeros -> simulate -> modify compensation -> measure hardware -> iterate to robust stability.

  • SMPS control loop + contract OR consultant OR interim;
  • stability and compensation + DC-DC OR power supply;
  • control loop tuning + PFC OR converter;
  • Type II OR Type III + compensation OR compensator;
  • pole zero + power supply OR converter;
  • Bode + SMPS OR DC-DC OR loop gain;
  • phase margin + power electronics;
  • frequency response + power supply + engineer;
  • LTspice OR PLECS OR PSIM + control loop OR stability;
  • transient response + power supply + debug OR optimisation;
  • unstable power supply OR oscillating converter + consultant OR troubleshooting;
  • power supply bring-up + contract;
  • PFC control loop + contract engineer;
  • TL431 OR optocoupler + compensation;
  • digital power + controller coefficients OR loop stability.

Do not require the advert to say Type II. The highest-value current contract signal in this pass uses the more general phrase SMPS control loops, stability and compensation.

Caveats

  • This is a micro-lens inside the broader electronics/power-engineering landscape, not a claim of broad specialist power-converter identity across every topology/power level.
  • Distinguish control-loop stabilisation / investigation / simulation / validation from roles whose dominant requirement is clean-sheet high-power converter architecture, magnetics design or traction-inverter ownership.
  • High-voltage work can introduce safety/process requirements outside the preferred bounded troubleshooting scope; assess role by role.
  • Aerospace/space evidence requires project-level civilian/non-military screening before inclusion as an actionable lead.
  • Avanti Recruitment, Roc Search Europe, Computer Futures and Circuit32 Recruitment are recruiter/intermediary evidence sources. Their anonymous clients must remain anonymous until independently attributable.
  • Specialist consultancies may sell precisely this work without themselves hiring contractors. Treat technical fit and contractor-channel evidence as separate claims.
  • Encortec's current listing permits freelance/consultant-style engagement, but IR35 status and any Ltd-company/PSC route are unverified.
  • Job-board dates/status can decay quickly. Current claims above are dated to this 2026-09-03 pass; preserve expired adverts only as historical market evidence.
  • Bode/Type II/III terminology is often absent from job titles. Search by failure mode, control-loop problem and simulation/measurement workflow rather than title alone.
