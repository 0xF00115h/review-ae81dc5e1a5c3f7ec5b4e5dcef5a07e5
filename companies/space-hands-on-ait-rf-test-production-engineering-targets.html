Why interesting

This lane deliberately excludes generic aerospace and process-heavy systems engineering unless there is evidence of real bench ownership. The attractive part of the space market is where hardware has reached the point where it must actually work: spacecraft and payload integration, RF verification, production test, harnesses, EGSE/GSE, environmental qualification, PCB fault isolation, anomaly resolution, NPI and test automation.

The selection rule is therefore work-shape rather than sector prestige. Strong targets show scopes, network/spectrum analysers, test racks, harnesses, solder/rework, scripts, cleanroom integration, thermal-vacuum/vibration testing, production debugging or system-level fault finding. ECSS/configuration-control overhead is expected, but the engineering must still involve physical access and measurable technical ownership.

Fresh research on 2 September 2026 finds a genuine hands-dirty submarket with both permanent and contractor routes. SSTL is the clearest direct contractor example; Open Cosmos, AAC Clyde Space, Spire, NewOrbit, CesiumAstro, Alba Orbital and Oxford Space Systems are strong employer/work-shape targets; Celestia UK and EECL are especially interesting on the RF/ground-test/ATE side.

Working-style fit

Surrey Satellite Technology Ltd (SSTL) — strongest direct contractor + pragmatic hands-on signal. SSTL currently lists multiple contractor roles in AIT, including AIT Harness Engineer - Contractor and ESA Qualified Inspector - Contractor; its careers system also retains AIT Integration, flight assembly and production-test contractor role families. A recent AIT Integration Engineer contractor posting was explicitly available through an outsourcing partner and accepted HND/HNC or four years of demonstrable hands-on experience. The person specification asked for a broad mechanical/electrical/electronics background, practical hands-on ability, RF communications as an advantage and a flexible and pragmatic attitude. This is one of the cleanest literal matches between space work and the preferred engineering style.

Open Cosmos — exceptionally strong bench/production work shape; current route is permanent. The Harwell Electronics Engineer – Satellite Manufacturing role sits inside AIT Production and explicitly covers integrating and validating electronic assemblies, NPI, diagnostic/test setups, PCB troubleshooting and repair, production support, test data analysis and manufacturability/testability. Open Cosmos describes AIT as the workshop where designs become physical satellites and currently has multiple AIT/production roles across Europe. This is much closer to product bring-up and sustaining engineering than conventional space systems work.

AAC Clyde Space — very strong cross-sector/background flexibility plus electronics/test ownership. Current Glasgow electronics hiring includes analogue/digital electronics, embedded interfaces, DFM/DFT, Python/C/C++, hands-on diagnosis/debug of new and existing designs and guiding test teams. The role accepts a relevant degree or equivalent work experience; space/CubeSat experience is desirable rather than mandatory, and the advert explicitly welcomes engineers from regulated industries who want to grow their knowledge and get involved and learn from the project. A current Manufacturing Test Engineer role is explicitly hands-on, testing and debugging electronic assemblies and spacecraft systems. This is one of the strongest learn the space domain while being useful immediately signals in the pass.

Alba Orbital — strongest small-company/problem-first culture signal. Alba builds PocketQube spacecraft and currently advertises electronics/manufacturing roles plus speculative engineering. Its first-party careers material explicitly says applicants from all backgrounds and experiences should apply even if they do not meet every listed requirement. The company describes itself as small, fast and ownership-heavy: engineers may design a subsystem, rework an existing design to make a deadline, learn quickly because there is no manual, and own real projects from the first week. Separate manufacturing material includes radio ground stations, sensitive RF test equipment, soldering, test software and a requirement to get stuck in. Contractor route is not verified, but employer resonance is exceptionally high.

NewOrbit Space — very strong lean-team AIT ownership; current roles permanent. Current Reading Satellite AIT work owns the flow from engineering model through flight acceptance, GSE and electrical/mechanical/system test campaigns. Because the team is lean, AIT also absorbs quality and goods-in responsibilities and explicitly expects the engineer to get hands-on. The work is somewhat more procedural than the ideal bench-debug role, but the organisational shape is unusually broad and practical for space.

Spire Global, Glasgow — strong RF/electronics bench fit inside a larger company. Current Spacecraft Electronics work includes custom sensors, radios, power systems, PCBAs, third-party fabricators, proof-of-concept through NPI and last-minute fixes. The current Senior RF Engineer role is even more literal: RF module production/test, bench-level fault finding, EMC/thermal-vacuum/spacecraft-level testing, test plans, RF instruments and tuning. This is a strong hands-dirty space lane despite the larger-company context.

CesiumAstro UK — extremely strong RF/test fit, but credential/export constraints are heavier. The current Milton Keynes Communications Test Engineer role explicitly describes the organisation as hands-on, interactive and autonomous. The work owns RF/baseband modem verification, measurement setups, calibration/metrology, Python test software and troubleshooting across DSP/HW/SW boundaries using signal generators, analysers, VNAs and power sensors. The caveat is more conventional filtering: degree requirements, specific RF measurement experience, export controls and some security restrictions.

Oxford Space Systems — very strong MAIT/learning resonance; contractor route unknown. First-party recruitment material describes MAIT staff moving from early concepts to flight hardware, including batteries, RF, mechanisms and calibration systems. The company explicitly presents an open environment that supports growth and learning, with breadboarding and hands-on manufacturing/integration visible in the careers material. The environment looks materially more practical than a conventional large-aerospace programme, but no current contractor route was verified.

Celestia UK — high-value ground-segment / RF-test route. Celestia designs ground stations, gateway technologies, RF/digital processing and satellite test systems. Its services explicitly include custom test systems, RF/microwave testing and hardware-in-the-loop simulation, and the UK team has dedicated design/test facilities plus a Head of MAIT. This is strategically important because it captures the hands-dirty side of space without requiring flight-hardware design: antennas, RF chains, payload simulation, ground equipment, test racks and verification. Current individual contractor use remains unverified.

EECL — unusually literal RF payload test/ATE consultancy, but defence exposure requires project-level screening. EECL's current Electronics & Software Engineer role combines analogue/digital/power boards, Python/C++, rack-mounted automated test equipment and ownership of hardware bring-up, testing and debugging in the lab. In February 2026 EECL won a £1.5m ESA contract to provide ground support equipment and qualification testing for a digital satellite payload, including TVAC, vibration, EMC and automated test systems; a January case study describes automated RF payload test racks covering 18 ports from 10–30 GHz. This is excellent technical resonance and a proven external engineering-services route, but the company also works in defence, so only clearly civilian/space assignments should be pursued.

Astroscale UK — useful AIT target with mixed programme caveat. Current Didcot hiring includes Mechanical AIT and Senior Digital Electronics roles, and the company explicitly frames itself around pioneering spacecraft and learning from successes and failures. The core civil-space-sustainability mission is attractive, but Astroscale also currently advertises a Defence Flight Dynamics role. Treat it as a project-screened space employer rather than assuming the whole organisation is civilian-only.

Current route

Treat Space — hands-on AIT / RF / test / production as a distinct market segment, separate from general aerospace. Search by physical work rather than the word space alone:

  • AIT, AIV, MAIT, spacecraft integration, payload integration;
  • RF payload test, communications test, RF verification, satcom test;
  • EGSE, GSE, ground support equipment, payload simulator, HIL;
  • production test, manufacturing test, NPI, flight hardware, test lab;
  • TVAC, thermal vacuum, vibration, EMC, environmental qualification when paired with troubleshooting/test ownership;
  • harness, test cable, PCB repair, fault finding, non-conformance, anomaly resolution;
  • ground station, gateway, RF test system, ATE for the ground-side lane.

Caveats

  • Do not broaden this into generic aerospace. Space is included only where the work is bench-right: physical integration, electronics/RF test, production/NPI, EGSE/GSE, fault isolation or hands-on qualification.
  • AIT can still become documentation/review heavy. Distinguish engineers who execute/debug tests from roles that mainly author procedures and manage reviews.
  • Flight hardware implies configuration control, cleanliness, traceability and formal test procedures. These are acceptable overheads only where substantial practical engineering remains.
  • Separate independent PSC contracting from fixed-term employment, outsourcing-partner contingent work and consultancy/service-company delivery.
  • Do not infer anonymous ReeVR or recruiter clients from Hampshire/Harwell geography or role fingerprints.
  • Space employers can have mixed civil/defence portfolios. Under the current policy, defence, military payloads, weapons, tactical systems and explicitly defence-cleared programmes remain excluded. Apply this project-level screen particularly to EECL, CesiumAstro, Celestia and Astroscale where mixed activity or security/export constraints exist.
  • Some current SSTL work requires recent EU-based personal security clearance and EU/Schengen citizenship; role-specific eligibility may therefore override technical fit.
  • NewOrbit's current AIT role includes substantial QMS/procedure ownership; treat it as broad practical ownership but not automatically as a bench-debug role.

Private notes

This is intentionally a Space lane, not an Aerospace lane. The durable identity signal is space hardware that has reached the point where it must actually work.

Highest-confidence immediate targets from this research are SSTL, AAC Clyde Space, Open Cosmos, Alba Orbital, Celestia UK, EECL, Spire and CesiumAstro. Oxford Space Systems, NewOrbit and Astroscale remain valuable resonators/watch targets.

Potential future canonical/Match review should resolve whether SSTL, AAC Clyde Space, Alba Orbital, NewOrbit, Spire, CesiumAstro, Oxford Space Systems, Celestia UK, EECL and Astroscale already exist in canonical identity before adding anything. Open Cosmos and Celestia UK are already known elsewhere in the jobsearch intelligence, so avoid duplicate identity creation.

This lane should rank companies on at least four separable dimensions: bench_hands_on, cross_domain_ownership, background_learning_flexibility, and contractor_accessibility. Process/formality should be a modifier rather than an automatic rejection because flight hardware inevitably carries more traceability than ordinary electronics development.
