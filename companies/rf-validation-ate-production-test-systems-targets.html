Why interesting

This lane isolates one of the strongest cross-sector work shapes in the market: engineers who own the measurement system as well as the DUT problem. The characteristic work is not routine pass/fail testing. It is defining how performance will be measured, building fixtures and racks, controlling instruments and DUTs, automating sequences, debugging measurement setups, improving coverage, reducing false failures and tracing failures across RF, analogue/digital electronics, firmware, interfaces and production processes.

The lane spans three related patterns:

  • RF validation / characterisation — S-parameters, gain/noise/linearity, sensitivity, TX/RX performance, spectrum/VNA/power measurements, 3GPP-style verification, RF bring-up and measurement-driven design feedback;
  • ATE / test-system development — fixtures, ITAs, harnesses, switching, DAQ, programmable instruments, Python/LabVIEW/TestStand/C/C++, DUT control, manufacturing-test architecture and commissioning;
  • production-test / failure resolution — NPI, pilot builds, yield, false failures, component-level diagnosis, field/production returns, root cause, engineering changes and sustaining support.

The durable selection criterion is: own the measurement method and fault isolation, not merely execute somebody else's test procedure. This work is highly portable across wireless, semiconductor, photonics, scientific instruments, medical devices, space, industrial electronics, IoT and general product manufacturing.

A second editorial/verification pass on 2 September 2026 found the contractor evidence to be robust, corrected several ageing permanent-role links, removed one dead Forefront vacancy link, and added stronger current first-party examples from Oxford Instruments, Semtech and Fractile.

Caveats

  • Distinguish test-system ownership from routine test execution. Roles dominated by following procedures and recording pass/fail results should rank much lower unless they include significant fault isolation, method development or test-equipment ownership.
  • Current is reserved for roles still present on a first-party board or independently retrievable as a current/recent vacancy. Expired LinkedIn redirects are no longer used as evidence of current demand.
  • LabVIEW is common but should not become an accidental hard filter. The durable capability is instrument/DUT control, state management, automation, data capture and diagnostic reasoning; implementations may use Python, C/C++, C#, TestStand or proprietary frameworks.
  • RF-validation roles can drift into narrow compliance/certification work. Prioritise measurement-driven design feedback, bring-up, characterisation and troubleshooting.
  • Production-test roles can become technician-only. Prefer roles that own fixtures, test architecture, NPI, root cause, yield and engineering changes.
  • Preserve recruiter-client attribution grades. The Cambridge RF/PMR advert remains strongly Sepura-shaped but not recruiter-confirmed; SoCode/Platform/KO2 ATE clients remain anonymous.
  • Do not allow defence-sector ATE volume to overwhelm the civilian search. Defence/weapon/cleared programmes remain excluded even where the test work is technically excellent.
  • Semiconductor/photonics roles such as Semtech and Salience can be highly test-centric but may demand domain depth beyond the preferred generalist profile; treat them as stretch unless the role explicitly values transferable experience.
  • Test-equipment manufacturers and integrators can be excellent targets, but distinguish building test products for customers from owning DUT/product validation internally; both fit, but the commercial/work rhythm differs.
